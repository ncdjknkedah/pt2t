Treatment threshold: persistent ≥140/90. ACE-I or ARB is first line; do not combine ACE-I with ARB. The NDR audit KPI counts BP <140/80.

Very high riskEstablished CVD (CHD, stroke, PAD) OR target organ damage (albuminuria/proteinuria, eGFR <30, LVH, retinopathy) OR ≥3 major risk factors (age ≥65, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, smoking, obesity BMI ≥27.5)<1.4<2.2

    
High riskDiabetes ≥10 years without target organ damage + ≥1 other risk factor. Also applied to patients who do not meet the moderate-risk criteria (see note).<1.8<2.6

    
Moderate riskAge <50, diabetes <10 years, no other risk factors<2.6<3.4

    

    
Source: CPG T2DM 2020 Table 3-26 (adapted from Cosentino et al., 2020); definitions of target organ damage and major risk factors follow ESC/EASD 2019. Patients who fit no category (e.g. age ≥50 with diabetes <10 years) are placed in high risk as a prudent approach. If the target cannot be reached, aim for ≥50% reduction from pre-treatment level. All patients >40 years should receive a statin regardless of LDL-C. Very complex older adults: individualised target; continue statin while tolerated.

  

  

    
Glycaemic treatment logic

    
HbA1c gapAction (CPG Figure 3-4)

    
0.5–1% above targetAdd 1 drug from a different class, OR intensify insulin if already on insulin

    
1–2% above targetConsider 2 drugs from different classes, OR intensify insulin

    
>2% above targetStart insulin ± GLP-1 RA, OR intensify insulin

    

    
Monitoring: HbA1c every 6 months (cost constraint); in between, titration decisions are guided by capillary FBS or SMBG every 2–4 weeks. Fasting target 4.4–7.0 mmol/L (CPG Table 3-5); older adults 5.0–7.2 (healthy), 5.0–8.3 (complex), 5.6–10.0 (very complex) (CPG Table 6-2). Post-prandial: 4.4–8.5 mmol/L. Insulin (CPG Figure 3-2): basal 10 U or 0.1–0.2 U/kg; titrate on fasting glucose; reduce 10–20% if hypoglycaemia. If HbA1c remains above target although FPG is at target, or basal dose >0.5 U/kg: add prandial insulin 4 U or 10% of basal dose, titrate 1–2 U or 10–15% every 3 days. Total dose >1.5–2.0 U/kg: look for causes (adherence, technique, infection). Stop SU once on full insulin. Glibenclamide is not recommended for age >60. Drug choice by patient profile (CPG Figure 3-5): ASCVD (SGLT2-i / GLP-1 RA), heart failure (SGLT2-i), DKD (SGLT2-i; do not start if eGFR <30), obesity (GLP-1 RA / SGLT2-i), hypoglycaemia risk (DPP-4i / SGLT2-i). Metformin: half dose at eGFR 30–44, stop if eGFR <30. Drug availability is subject to the MOH Medicines Formulary and prescriber category.
